❓ Credit Card Rewards FAQ

What is the 4 MPD rule in Singapore?

The 4 MPD (miles per dollar) rule refers to using specialized credit cards that offer 4 miles for every S$1 spent on specific categories like online shopping or dining, rather than a base rate of 1.2-1.4 MPD.

Are air miles better than cashback?

Miles are generally better for high-value travel (Business/First class), while cashback is better for immediate savings and low-to-mid level spenders who don't travel often.

How do I avoid credit card annual fees?

Most banks in Singapore allow you to waive annual fees if you meet a minimum spend or call their customer service hotline. Some cards like HSBC Revolution have no annual fee at all.

💡 Pro Strategies for 2026

The "Amaze" Hack

Link your Citi Rewards card to Instarem Amaze. You get 4 mpd on overseas spending with better exchange rates and no 3.25% bank FCY fee.

The 4 MPD Rule

In Singapore, if you aren't earning at least 4 miles per dollar on specialized categories, you're leaving money on the table. Use dedicated category cards!

Watch the Caps

Most high-reward cards have monthly caps (e.g., $1,000 or $2,000). Rotate your spending to another card once you hit the limit.

The Yuu Ecosystem

Pair the DBS yuu card with the yuu app at merchants like Giant, Cold Storage, and 7-Eleven to earn up to 18% rebate or 9 miles per dollar. It's the highest yield in SG right now.

Avoid $5 Multiples

Be careful with UOB and OCBC cards; they award points in blocks of S$5. If you spend S$4.90, you earn zero points. Stick to DBS or Citi for small transactions.

Hospital & School Fees

Most cards exclude these. Use StanChart Journey or Amex True Cashback as they are among the few that still award points for healthcare and education spending.

Tax & Rent (PayAll/CardUp)

When you can't earn direct rewards on big bills, use Citi PayAll or CardUp. You'll pay a ~1.7% to 2% fee, but it's often the cheapest way to "buy" large amounts of miles (around 1.5 cents each).
